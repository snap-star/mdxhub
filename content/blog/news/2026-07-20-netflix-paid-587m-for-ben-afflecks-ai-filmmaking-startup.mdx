Netflix has finally put a number on one of the more closely watched deals in the intersection of Hollywood and artificial intelligence. According to a fresh regulatory filing, the streaming company paid $587 million in cash to acquire InterPositive, a startup co-founded by actor and director Ben Affleck. The acquisition itself was announced back in March, but at the time Netflix declined to disclose the financial terms, leaving observers to speculate about the true size of the bet.

### What InterPositive does

The startup builds AI tools aimed squarely at filmmakers rather than audiences. Affleck has framed the technology as an assistive layer for post-production, describing tools that help creators refine and improve footage after shooting has wrapped. When the deal was first revealed, he pointedly cast the effort as a way to "protect the power of human creativity" — an attempt to position AI as a collaborator that augments craft instead of replacing the people who make films.

As part of the acquisition, Netflix said the entire InterPositive team would join the company, with Affleck himself coming aboard as a senior advisor. A subsequent Bloomberg report had hinted the deal might be substantial, but the newly surfaced filing confirms just how much Netflix was willing to spend to bring the tooling and talent in-house.

### Why the price tag matters

A $587 million all-cash outlay is a strong signal of how seriously Netflix views generative and assistive AI as part of its production pipeline. The company has not been shy about its use of the technology: in its most recent earnings report, Netflix said roughly 300 of its titles have already employed generative AI in some form. Buying a filmmaking-focused startup outright, rather than simply licensing software, suggests Netflix wants tighter control over the tools its productions rely on and the ability to shape their development internally.

The move also lands in a politically charged moment for Hollywood. AI in filmmaking remains a flashpoint, with unions, writers, and performers wary of how the technology could erode jobs and creative control. By foregrounding a well-known director like Affleck and emphasizing "human creativity," Netflix appears to be trying to get ahead of the backlash, framing its investment as one that empowers filmmakers rather than sidelines them.

### The bigger picture

Whether that framing holds up will depend on how the tools are actually deployed across Netflix's slate. Assistive post-production features — cleaning up footage, smoothing effects, or accelerating tedious editing tasks — are far less contentious than fully generated performances or synthetic actors. For now, the acquisition cements Netflix as one of the most aggressive spenders on AI infrastructure among the major streamers, and it gives the company a marquee name to help sell that vision both inside and outside the industry. The disclosed price ensures the deal will be scrutinized closely as a benchmark for what studios are prepared to pay to own their AI stack.
