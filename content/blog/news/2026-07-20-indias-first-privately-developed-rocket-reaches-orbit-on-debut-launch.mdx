India's private space industry reached a landmark milestone this weekend as Skyroot Aerospace's Vikram-1 rocket climbed to orbit on its very first flight — a feat that has eluded most new orbital vehicles on their debut. The launch marks the first time a privately developed Indian rocket has reached orbit, and it did so on the opening attempt, something the company's leadership admitted they scarcely believed was possible.

### A dramatic ascent

The Vikram-1 lifted off from India's primary spaceport on Sriharikota Island at 1:35 am EDT (06:35 UTC) on Saturday, around midday local time along the country's southeastern coast. The launch was pushed back more than half an hour to resolve a last-minute technical issue before the vehicle finally left the pad.

The rocket is modest in scale next to India's larger government workhorses. Standing about 72 feet (22 meters) tall, Vikram-1 can loft payloads of up to roughly 770 pounds (350 kilograms) into low-Earth orbit. Its flight profile relied on three solid-fueled motors firing in succession to punch through the atmosphere, followed by a small liquid-fueled fourth stage that ignited and accelerated the payload to orbital velocity of around 17,000 mph. Onboard cameras streamed live views of the ascent.

The flight went off without major problems, though there was one moment of intrigue: during separation of the third stage from the fourth, the spent third-stage motor appeared to linger near the upper stage during a brief coast rather than backing cleanly away. Despite that quirk, the mission continued to a successful orbital insertion.

### "Nothing short of a suspense movie"

Skyroot's cofounder and CEO Pawan Kumar Chandana was visibly moved in remarks to his launch team. "We achieved one of the biggest milestones ever in India's space sector — the first private orbital rocket reaching orbit on the very first attempt," he said. "On the first attempt, reaching orbit, I never thought it was possible. Skyroot's team made it possible." He described the launch as "nothing short of a suspense movie."

### Beating a tough track record

First flights of new privately built orbital rockets are notoriously unforgiving. SpaceX needed four tries before its Falcon 1 reached orbit in 2008, and Rocket Lab's Electron fell short on its own debut in 2017. Against that backdrop, Skyroot's clean first-attempt success stands out, and it positions the company as a serious contender in the growing global market for small-satellite launch services.

The achievement also underscores how India's space ecosystem is maturing beyond its state agency, ISRO, whose officials attended the post-launch celebration. A thriving private launch sector could give Indian and international customers a lower-cost, homegrown option for deploying small payloads, and Saturday's flight suggests that ambition is quickly becoming reality.
