> **source from:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/967612/google-pixel-11a-tensor-g6-leak)

Google's next affordable phone, the Pixel 11a, may return to using a current-generation processor after a leak indicated it will ship with the same Tensor G6 chip expected in Google's flagship models. The claim, attributed to the leaker Mystic Leaks, would mark a course correction from the Pixel 10a, which disappointed some buyers by shipping with the older Tensor G4 instead of the Tensor G5 found in the Pixel 10 and 10 Pro.

That earlier decision broke with the usual formula for Google's "a" series. Historically, the budget Pixels kept the same modern silicon as their flagship siblings while trimming costs elsewhere, such as materials, cameras, or charging speeds. Reverting to a year-old chip on the 10a felt like a step backward, so a Pixel 11a built around the latest Tensor G6 would restore the value proposition that made the a-series appealing in the first place.

### What the chip change could mean

According to the leak, the Tensor G6 is rumored to reuse the same PowerVR DXT-48-1536 GPU as the Tensor G5, so raw graphics performance may not leap forward dramatically. Even so, it would represent a meaningful upgrade over the Mali-G715 GPU in the Tensor G4 that powered the 10a. The more consequential change may be under the hood rather than in benchmarks.

The report suggests the Tensor G6 moves away from Samsung's Exynos modems in favor of a MediaTek M90 modem. That switch could directly target two problems that have dogged Tensor-powered Pixels for years: excessive battery drain and dropped or unreliable signal. Both issues have been persistent complaints across multiple Pixel generations, and a more efficient, more dependable modem could improve everyday experience in ways that spec sheets do not fully capture.

### Why it matters

For prospective buyers, the modem change may end up mattering more than the processor label. Connectivity and battery life are the kinds of fundamentals that shape daily satisfaction with a phone, and Tensor chips have historically lagged rivals on exactly those fronts. If Google can pair flagship-class silicon with a modem that finally addresses signal and power efficiency, the Pixel 11a could be one of the more compelling mid-range Android options.

As always, these details come from a leak and not from Google, so the specifics could shift before any official announcement. Chip configurations, modem choices, and final performance can all change during development, and rumors about unreleased hardware are inherently uncertain. Still, the report lines up with a plausible strategy for Google: undo the perceived downgrade of the 10a and give the budget line a stronger technical foundation. If it holds up, the Pixel 11a would signal that Google is once again treating its affordable phone as a genuine flagship-adjacent device rather than a compromise built around leftover parts.
