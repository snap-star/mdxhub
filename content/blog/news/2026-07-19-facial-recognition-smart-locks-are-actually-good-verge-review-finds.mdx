Facial recognition on a front-door lock has long sounded like an over-engineered gimmick, but The Verge's smart-home reviewer Jennifer Pattison Tuohy came away convinced the technology has matured. After testing four locks that unlock with a glance, she concluded that while true hands-free entry using ultra-wideband (UWB) is still the superior experience, facial recognition is now worth considering for anyone who wants a genuinely futuristic front door.

### From gimmick to genuinely useful

The core appeal is convenience: walk up, be recognized, and step inside without fumbling for keys, a phone, or a code. The review frames face unlock as delivering the "sci-fi experience" homeowners imagine when they picture a smart home, and finds that the current crop of devices largely lives up to it in day-to-day use. That's a meaningful shift from earlier attempts, which tended to be slow, unreliable, or awkward enough that most people fell back on a keypad or an app.

Tuohy's comparison sizes up the competing locks on how quickly and accurately they identify an approaching resident, alongside the usual smart-lock considerations that determine whether a device is actually worth installing on your only entry point.

### The UWB caveat

Even as face unlock wins her cautious endorsement, the review is clear that hands-free UWB unlocking remains better where it's available, letting the door recognize your presence without requiring you to stop and present your face to a sensor. Facial recognition, then, is positioned as a strong option rather than the definitive best approach, especially for buyers drawn to the novelty and the seamless feel it provides.

### Why it matters

Smart locks sit at a sensitive intersection of convenience, security, and privacy, and adding a camera that maps your face raises the stakes on all three. A face-unlocking lock that finally works reliably could push biometric entry from a niche curiosity toward the mainstream, the way fingerprint and face unlock did on smartphones. But it also invites the questions that come with any always-on camera at your threshold: where the facial data is stored, how it's protected, and who else can access it.

The Verge's verdict is a useful signal for shoppers weighing a purchase this year. The hardware has caught up to the marketing, and for the right buyer the experience is legitimately impressive, but the recommendation comes with the reminder that a lock is only as good as the security and privacy practices behind it. As biometric front doors become more common, those trade-offs will matter as much as how quickly the lock recognizes your face.
