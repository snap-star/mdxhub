For anyone who still treasures a shelf of game cases, the last week brought a pair of gut punches. Two industry giants took steps that, taken together, look like the beginning of the end for physical video games — and the implications for consumer rights, resale markets, and game preservation are serious.

### GTA VI ships without a disc

On June 24th, Rockstar Games finally attached a price to Grand Theft Auto VI ahead of its scheduled November 19th release on PS5 and Xbox Series X/S: $79.99 for the base edition and $99.99 for the Ultimate Edition. The prices weren't shocking — even Nintendo has flirted with $79.99 pricing on marquee titles. What raised eyebrows was the format. GTA VI will be sold digitally, with any boxed version amounting to a download code rather than a disc.

Selling a game as a code in a box isn't new, but GTA VI is by far the biggest title to do it. Because one of the most anticipated releases in years is skipping discs, Rockstar's decision could nudge other publishers to follow suit. And an all-digital release means players can't buy used copies, resell their own, or lend a game to a friend — long-standing perks of physical ownership that quietly disappear.

### Sony drops an even bigger bomb

Just days after Rockstar's announcement, Sony went further. Starting in January 2028, it said, it will no longer manufacture physical discs for any new PlayStation games. The backlash was swift and loud: critics piled on across social media, and even Sony's own accounts went quiet for days amid the outcry.

Retailers and preservationists were among the sharpest critics. As one industry figure put it, the shift is "unfortunate news for those who still prefer buying games on physical media" and "a significant hit to consumer rights, the resale market, and game creators whose businesses rely on the physical market." Sony, for its part, framed the move as simply following demand, calling it "a natural direction" given that "the general preference for digital media significantly outpaces physical discs."

### The digital reality

There's no denying the trend. Players overwhelmingly buy digital these days, drawn by convenience and the fact that console makers have improved backward compatibility across generations. Some gamers can't even play discs, since disc-less versions of the PS5 and Xbox Series consoles exist. The author admits to going mostly digital himself since the Switch era.

But convenience carries hidden costs. Digital storefronts can pull titles over expired licenses or shut down entirely, taking purchased games with them. GTA VI will almost certainly stay widely available given its scale, but smaller titles are far more vulnerable to vanishing.

### What's at stake

The concern isn't just nostalgia. Without discs, preservation grows harder, resale evaporates, and "ownership" increasingly means a revocable license. Whether GTA VI even hits its November 19th date — and whether it ever appears on disc — remains to be seen. But the direction is unmistakable, and for fans of physical media, the ground is shifting fast.
