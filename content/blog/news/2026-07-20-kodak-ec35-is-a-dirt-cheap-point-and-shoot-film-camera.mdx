The film photography revival keeps rolling along, and it's getting cheaper. Reto Project, the company behind a growing line of affordable analog cameras, is releasing the Kodak EC35, a 35mm point-and-shoot that costs just $34.99. Following the success of the $99 Kodak-branded Snapic A1, the EC35 pushes the entry price for shooting real film down to nearly impulse-purchase territory.

### Bare-bones by design

Nobody should mistake the EC35 for a serious photographic tool, and that's the point. It pairs a 25mm acrylic lens with a fixed f/10 aperture and a single 1/100 shutter speed, specs that essentially lock the camera into "shoot in bright light and hope for the best" mode. There's no autofocus, no exposure control, and none of the manual dials that enthusiasts fuss over. It is, in the truest sense, a point-and-shoot.

Compared to Reto's pricier A1, the EC35 strips things back even further. There's no double-exposure mode and no zone focusing to speak of. The camera is deliberately engineered to demand almost no thought from the user — load film, wind, press the shutter, and move on. That simplicity is arguably the whole appeal for a newcomer intimidated by the idea of shooting analog.

### One small flourish

The EC35 isn't entirely featureless. Its standout touch is a sliding lens cover reminiscent of the beloved Olympus Stylus (Mju) compacts, a small nod to classic point-and-shoot design that also helps protect the lens when the camera is tossed in a bag. Reto is also leaning into the fun factor with a range of colorways, including an avocado-green option that gives the otherwise utilitarian device a bit of retro personality.

### Why it matters

Film photography has enjoyed a sustained resurgence in recent years, driven in part by younger shooters drawn to the tactile, unhurried nature of analog and the distinctive look of film grain. But the hobby has also grown expensive: film stock, developing, and even entry-level reusable cameras can add up quickly, and vintage compacts have skyrocketed on the secondhand market.

A brand-new, Kodak-branded camera at under $35 lowers that barrier dramatically. It won't deliver the sharpness or flexibility of a proper SLR or a coveted vintage compact, but that's not the audience. The EC35 is aimed at curious beginners who want to try shooting film without committing a large sum upfront — and if it hooks them, they can graduate to something more capable later. In a market where "affordable" analog gear often still means triple-digit prices, Reto's latest is a reminder that the film renaissance can be genuinely accessible.
