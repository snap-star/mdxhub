> **source from:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/google-is-working-on-a-new-ai-chip-designed-to-make-gemini-more-efficient/)

Alphabet, Google's parent company, is designing a new server chip intended to run its in-house Gemini models more efficiently. The chip, reportedly known internally as "Frozen v2," is slated to arrive sometime in 2028, according to The Information, which cited anonymous sources. The report claims the chip could be six to ten times more efficient than Google's current AI hardware, measured by tokens generated per unit of power.

Google did not directly confirm the report to TechCrunch, but it did not deny it either. "Our teams are constantly researching and experimenting with new innovations to deliver maximum performance and efficiency," the company said, emphasizing its "full stack approach" of co-designing hardware and software while noting that not every project reaches production.

### The race to build custom silicon

The effort fits a broader industry pattern. AI companies increasingly want their own chips, both to run their models more efficiently and to ease the global crunch in AI computing capacity. Efficiency has become a central selling point as worries about AI spending have cooled some of the market euphoria that once surrounded the sector. At the same time, major players are trying to reduce their dependence on Nvidia, whose long-standing dominance in AI chips has left model makers reliant on its hardware.

Google is far from alone. In June, OpenAI unveiled its first custom chip, an inference processor called Jalapeño. Earlier this month, reports surfaced that Anthropic was in talks with Samsung about a new chipmaking partnership.

### Why investors are watching

Alphabet's spending has been a source of investor anxiety. Earlier this year, Google said it planned to spend between $180 billion and $190 billion, and with that much capital at stake, the company needs to show the investments will pay off. News of a dramatically more efficient chip appears to have reassured the market: following The Information's report, Alphabet's stock climbed roughly 3% on Monday morning, giving the company a boost ahead of its earnings report later in the week.

### Why it matters

If the efficiency gains hold up, Frozen v2 could meaningfully lower the cost of running Gemini at scale while reducing Google's reliance on outside suppliers. For now it remains an unconfirmed, years-out project, but the report underscores how the competitive edge in AI is shifting from model architecture alone toward the economics of the silicon underneath.
